Arizona has one of the oldest and broadest E-Verify mandates in the country. If you hire people here, you are required to use it. But when part of your workforce comes through a staffing agency, a lot of managers are unsure where their responsibility ends and the agency's begins.

This piece walks through the basics: what Arizona requires, how Form I-9 and E-Verify fit together, and how verification works when you bring in temporary workers.

## What Arizona law requires

Under the Legal Arizona Workers Act, **all Arizona employers must use E-Verify for employees hired after December 31, 2007.** That applies regardless of company size. Employers seeking state economic development incentives or government contracts face additional E-Verify requirements.

The enforcement teeth are real. According to the Arizona Attorney General's office, a county attorney can bring action to suspend or revoke the business licenses of an employer that knowingly or intentionally employs an unauthorized worker. Using E-Verify creates a rebuttable presumption that an employer did not knowingly hire an unauthorized worker, and good-faith compliance with the federal I-9 process can serve as an affirmative defense.

## Form I-9 and E-Verify are two different things

People often use these terms interchangeably. They are not the same.

- **Form I-9** is the federal form every U.S. employer must complete for every new hire, documenting identity and work authorization. It is required nationwide.
- **E-Verify** is the online system, run by the Department of Homeland Security with the Social Security Administration, that compares information from the I-9 against government records.

In practice, you complete the I-9 first, then create the E-Verify case using that information. The standard federal timing is that the employee completes Section 1 by the first day of work, and the employer completes Section 2 within three business days of the start date. Always use the current edition of the form posted by USCIS; editions do expire.

## Who verifies a temp worker?

The short answer: **the employer of record.** When a staffing agency hires a worker, that agency is the one completing the I-9 and the E-Verify case.

E-Verify's own guidance for staffing agencies makes several points clear:

- Staffing agencies must create E-Verify cases for all their new hires.
- An agency cannot skip E-Verify because a client asks it to.
- An agency must not verify selectively, such as only for certain clients or certain workers. Verification has to be applied consistently.
- Agencies can set the employee's first day of employment either as the date the person is first assigned to a job or the date they accept an offer and enter the assignment pool, as long as they use that approach consistently. They should not leave the certification date or first day of employment blank.

For host employers, E-Verify's guidance is that you should confirm with your staffing agency that it has verified the workers it places with you. An agency can show proof of its enrollment by printing its E-Verify company information page.

## What host employers should ask

You do not complete I-9s for agency employees, but you should not take compliance on faith either. Here is what we would ask any staffing partner, including us:

1. **Are you enrolled in E-Verify, and can you show proof?**
2. **Do you verify every new hire, not just those going to certain clients?**
3. **When do you complete the I-9 and create the E-Verify case relative to the first assignment?**
4. **How do you handle a tentative nonconfirmation?** The process has specific steps and notice requirements for the employee. Your partner should know them cold.
5. **Do you complete I-9s in person or use the DHS alternative procedure for remote document review?** The remote option is available only to employers enrolled in E-Verify and in good standing, and it comes with its own rules.
6. **How do you store and retain I-9 records, and could you produce them in an audit?**
7. **What happens if you convert a temp to your direct employee?** When you hire someone directly, you become the employer and need to complete your own I-9 and E-Verify case. The agency's paperwork does not transfer to you.

## Common mistakes we see

A few patterns cause trouble for employers, with or without agencies:

- **Treating E-Verify as optional for small teams.** In Arizona, it is not.
- **Running E-Verify before a job offer.** E-Verify is for new hires, not applicant screening.
- **Asking for specific documents.** Employees choose which acceptable documents to present from the lists on the form. Requesting particular documents can create discrimination exposure.
- **Skipping re-verification tracking.** Some work authorizations expire. Employers need a system for tracking those dates without over-documenting.
- **Assuming a temp-to-hire carries over.** It does not. New employer, new I-9.

## Why this matters for operations, not just HR

When verification goes wrong, the problems often show up on the floor first: a worker can't start on time, a crew comes up short on day one, or a large group has to be re-processed. A staffing partner with tight onboarding moves verification ahead of the start date so your shift is not waiting on paperwork.

## The bottom line

Arizona expects every employer to verify every new hire. When you use a staffing agency, that job belongs to the agency as employer of record, but you should still confirm they are doing it consistently and correctly.
