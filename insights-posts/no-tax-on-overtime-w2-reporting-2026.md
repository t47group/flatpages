For employers with a lot of hourly overtime, and that describes most distribution centers, auction operations and dealership service departments in peak, year-end payroll has a new step this time. Starting with 2026 wages, the IRS requires employers to report "qualified overtime compensation" separately on Form W-2 so employees can claim the new federal overtime deduction.

If your payroll provider has not talked with you about it yet, now is the time. Here is what the rule requires and what to check before your final payroll of the year.

## The deduction in brief

The federal tax law enacted in 2025 created a deduction for qualified overtime compensation. According to the IRS:

- It is effective for tax years **2025 through 2028**.
- Eligible workers can deduct up to **$12,500** per return, or **$25,000** on a joint return.
- The deduction phases out for modified adjusted gross income above **$150,000** ($300,000 for joint filers).
- It is available whether or not the worker itemizes.

This is an income tax deduction the employee claims on their return. It does **not** change payroll taxes. The IRS says overtime is still subject to Social Security and Medicare tax for both employer and employee, and to income tax withholding.

## What counts as "qualified overtime"

This is where precision matters. The IRS defines qualified overtime compensation as the **premium portion** of overtime that the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) requires, the "half" in time and a half.

Under the general method in the IRS FAQs, it equals hours over 40 in a workweek, times one-half, times the employee's **FLSA regular rate**.

Example: an employee whose regular rate is $20 works 50 hours. They are paid $30 an hour for 10 overtime hours, or $300. The qualified overtime is only the premium: 10 x $10 = **$100**.

The IRS lists several things that **do not** qualify:

- **Overtime not required by the FLSA,** including premiums paid only because of state law, a union contract or your own policy. If you pay double time, only the FLSA-required portion counts.
- **Pay to FLSA-exempt employees,** such as salaried executive, administrative and professional staff.
- Pay to certain 20 percent owners who actively manage the business.

## Why your regular rate matters here

Because qualified overtime is based on the regular rate, anything that belongs in the regular rate affects the number you report. Nondiscretionary bonuses, such as peak attendance or production incentives, must be included in the regular rate under DOL rules. If your payroll system calculates overtime on base pay alone, both the overtime you paid and the amount you report on the W-2 may be wrong. We covered how bonuses change the regular rate in our post on peak-season incentives.

## What changes on the 2026 Form W-2

According to the IRS's 2026 instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3:

- **Box 12, code TT** is new and reports the total amount of qualified overtime compensation.
- Report the **full amount paid,** even if it exceeds the employee's deduction limit. The IRS FAQs give the example of $30,000 paid being reported in full.
- For tips, there is a separate new **box 12, code TP**, and **box 14b** for Treasury tipped occupation codes. Most of the industries we work with will not use these.

For 2025, the IRS did not require separate reporting and offered transition relief. **For 2026 and later, separate reporting is required.**

In rare cases where a worker is an FLSA employee but an independent contractor for tax purposes, the amount goes on Form 1099-NEC box 1d or 1099-MISC box 14 instead.

## Key deadlines and other 2026 changes

- **W-2 deadline:** The IRS instructions say 2026 Forms W-2 are due to the Social Security Administration and to employees by **February 1, 2027**.
- **1099-NEC threshold:** For payments after 2025, the reporting threshold rose from $600 to **$2,000**.
- **Penalties:** The IRS says penalty amounts for late or incorrect information returns increased for returns due after December 31, 2026.
- **Corrections:** If you find an error after filing, the IRS FAQs say to file a Form W-2c and furnish it to the employee as soon as possible.
- **Withholding:** Employers do not reduce income tax withholding for this deduction unless the employee submits an updated Form W-4.

## Steps to take before your last 2026 payroll

1. **Confirm your payroll system tracks the FLSA premium separately** for each non-exempt employee, and that it will populate box 12, code TT.
2. **Check your regular rate calculation,** especially if you paid peak bonuses, shift differentials or other incentives.
3. **Separate FLSA overtime from extra overtime.** If you pay daily overtime or double time by policy, make sure only the FLSA-required premium is reported.
4. **Review exempt classifications** so salaried exempt staff are not reported.
5. **Prepare employees for questions.** Some will expect their whole overtime check to be tax-free. A short explanation of what the W-2 shows, and a referral to a tax professional, can head off frustration in February.

## If you use temporary workers

For temps placed through a staffing agency, the agency is the employer of record and issues the W-2, including box 12, code TT. Your role is to make sure hours are approved accurately and that any incentives you funded were communicated to the agency so they are in the regular rate.

*This article is general information, not tax or legal advice. Rules may continue to be updated, so confirm details with your payroll provider, CPA or the IRS.*

## The bottom line

For 2026, qualified overtime goes in box 12, code TT on Form W-2, and it is only the FLSA-required premium based on the regular rate. Get your payroll setup right in December, and your W-2s will be ready by February 1.
