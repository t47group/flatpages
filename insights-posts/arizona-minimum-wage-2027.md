Every fall, Arizona employers get the same piece of news: the state minimum wage is going up in January. For 2027, the formula in state law points to about **$15.65 per hour**, up 50 cents from the current $15.15 (the Industrial Commission of Arizona publishes the official rate each fall, so confirm it once posted). That is not a headline-grabbing jump, but if you run a warehouse, an auction lane or a dealership service drive with a lot of hourly people, those 50 cents ripple through your budget in ways that are easy to underestimate.

Here is how the rate is set, what is confirmed, and how we suggest Phoenix-area operations leaders plan for it.

## How Arizona sets the minimum wage

Arizona's minimum wage is tied to inflation by state law. Under A.R.S. 23-363, the rate is adjusted every January 1 by the increase in the federal Consumer Price Index for all urban consumers (CPI-U, U.S. city average), measured from August of one year to August of the next. The increase is rounded to the nearest five cents.

That means the new number is effectively locked in once the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the August CPI report each September. The Industrial Commission of Arizona (ICA), which enforces the minimum wage law, then posts the official notice and updated poster. Last year the ICA published its 2026 notice on October 2.

## The 2027 math

The BLS reported on September 11, 2026 that CPI-U rose **3.4 percent** in the 12 months ending August 2026. Apply that to the current rate:

- 2026 rate: $15.15
- 3.4 percent increase: about 51.5 cents
- Rounded to the nearest nickel: 50 cents
- **Expected 2027 rate: about $15.65, effective January 1, 2027** (pending the official ICA notice)

The law also lets employers pay tipped employees up to $3.00 less per hour than the minimum, as long as wages plus tips meet the full minimum for every hour worked. For most of the industries we staff, tip credits do not apply, but it is worth knowing.

A note on local rates: Flagstaff and Tucson set their own, higher minimums. If you run crews outside the Phoenix metro, check those cities separately.

As of this writing, the ICA had not yet posted its formal 2027 notice. Watch the [ICA minimum wage page](https://www.azica.gov/labor-minimum-wage-main-page) for the official poster and confirm before you finalize payroll settings.

## Why a 50-cent raise costs more than 50 cents

If you only have a handful of people at the minimum, the direct cost is small. The real impact comes from three places.

### 1. Wage compression

Most warehouse and yard jobs in the Valley already pay above the state minimum. But when the floor rises, the gap between your entry-level rate and your experienced or lead rates shrinks. A forklift operator who earned $3 more than a new picker now earns $2.50 more. People notice. Many employers end up moving several pay bands, not just the bottom one.

### 2. Payroll burden

Every dollar of wages carries employer-side costs: payroll taxes, workers' compensation premiums (often calculated on payroll) and in some cases benefits tied to earnings. When you model the increase, model the fully loaded cost, not the hourly rate alone.

### 3. Overtime

Overtime is paid at one-and-a-half times the regular rate. If your peak weeks run heavy on overtime, a higher base rate raises that cost too.

## How to budget for 2027

Here is the approach we recommend to operations and HR leaders building next year's labor plan:

1. **Pull a rate census.** List every hourly role, its current rate and headcount. Flag anyone under $15.65 and anyone within roughly a dollar of it.
2. **Decide your compression policy.** Will you move only the people below the new floor, or adjust bands above it? Make the call deliberately rather than reacting when someone quits.
3. **Recalculate loaded cost per hour.** Include payroll taxes and comp. This is the number that belongs in your budget, not the posted wage.
4. **Revisit your staffing bill rates.** If you use a staffing partner, bill rates for roles near the minimum will move on January 1. Ask your agency for updated rates now so the change does not surprise your finance team.
5. **Update postings and systems.** New posters, offer letters, job ads and timekeeping rules should all reflect the new rate before the first payroll of 2027.
6. **Look at the competitive rate, not just the legal one.** In practice, the rate that fills shifts in Phoenix distribution is often set by what the large employers down the road are paying. The legal minimum is a floor, not a recruiting strategy.

## What this means if you use temporary labor

When you use a staffing agency, the agency is the employer of record and is responsible for paying at least the legal minimum. But the cost lands on you through the bill rate. A good partner will tell you ahead of time which assignments are affected and by how much.

We also suggest using the increase as a moment to check whether your temp pay rates are competitive enough to keep people showing up. A rate that was attractive in early 2026 may look ordinary once the new floor kicks in.

The 2027 increase is modest, predictable and already calculable. The employers who handle it well are the ones who build it into their Q4 budget instead of discovering it in January payroll.
