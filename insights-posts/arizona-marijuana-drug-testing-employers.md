Since Arizona voters approved recreational marijuana in 2020, we have heard a version of the same question from almost every client with forklifts, yard trucks or customer vehicles on site: can we still test, and what happens if someone tests positive?

The short answer is yes, employers in Arizona keep substantial rights. But the details depend on whether you have a written policy, whether the person is a medical marijuana cardholder and whether the job is safety-sensitive. Here is a practical overview for Phoenix-area operations.

## What Prop 207 does and does not change

The Smart and Safe Arizona Act (Proposition 207) is codified in A.R.S. Title 36, Chapter 28.2. Its text includes several provisions that preserve employer rights. According to A.R.S. 36-2851, the law:

- **Does not restrict employers' rights to maintain a drug- and alcohol-free workplace**
- **Does not affect employers' ability to adopt policies restricting marijuana use** by employees or prospective employees
- **Does not require employers to allow or accommodate marijuana use,** possession or cultivation in the workplace
- Allows property owners and businesses to prohibit or regulate otherwise legal conduct on their property

The same statute keeps it illegal to drive or operate a vehicle while impaired "to even the slightest degree" by marijuana. For dealership porters, auction drivers and anyone operating equipment, that matters.

In short, legal recreational use did not create a right to use marijuana at work or to show up impaired.

## Medical marijuana cardholders are different

Arizona's older medical marijuana law adds protections that recreational users do not have. Under A.R.S. 36-2813, unless an exception applies, an employer may not:

- Discriminate in hiring, termination or terms of employment based on someone's status as a registered cardholder
- Penalize a registered qualifying patient for a positive test for marijuana components or metabolites, **unless** the patient used, possessed or was impaired by marijuana on the premises or during work hours

There is also an exception when following these rules would cause an employer to lose a monetary or licensing-related benefit under federal law or regulations. Employers with federally regulated drivers or federal contracts should get advice on how that interacts with their programs.

The practical takeaway: a positive marijuana test alone is not always enough to act against a cardholder. You need to look at impairment and on-the-job use.

## Arizona's drug testing law: the policy matters

Arizona has a drug testing statute, A.R.S. 23-493 and following, that gives employers legal protection when they test under a compliant written policy. Under A.R.S. 23-493.04, the policy must be in writing and distributed to employees (or made available the same way as other personnel policies), and applicants must be told they will be tested. The policy should cover:

1. Your drug and alcohol policy statement
2. Who is subject to testing
3. When testing may happen
4. Which substances are tested
5. Testing methods and collection procedures
6. Consequences of refusing a test
7. Possible discipline based on results
8. The employee's right to get written results on request
9. The employee's right to confidentially explain a positive result
10. Your confidentiality policy for results

The statute allows testing for job-related reasons, including possible impairment, workplace accidents (for employees reasonably believed to have contributed, as soon as practicable), safety, productivity and reasonable suspicion. Random testing is permitted. And if you adopt an impairment testing policy, **all compensated employees, including supervisors and officers,** must be uniformly covered.

## Impairment and safety-sensitive roles

A.R.S. 23-493.06 protects employers who have a compliant policy when they act on a **good-faith belief** that an employee was impaired at work or used drugs on the premises or during work hours. It also allows employers to remove someone from a **safety-sensitive position**, by reassignment or leave, based on a good-faith belief of current drug use that could cause impairment, whether the drug is legal, prescribed or otherwise.

The statute lists what a good-faith belief can be based on, including test results, warning labels, the employee's own statements, a physician or pharmacist's advice and other reliable information.

For most of the roles we staff, from forklift operators to lot drivers, it is worth defining in your policy which positions you treat as safety-sensitive and why.

## Post-accident testing and OSHA

OSHA's injury reporting rules prohibit retaliating against employees for reporting injuries. In a 2018 memo, OSHA clarified that **most workplace drug testing is permissible,** including random testing, testing unrelated to injury reporting, testing required by state workers' compensation law or federal rules, and post-incident testing to find the root cause of an incident. OSHA said the employer should test everyone whose conduct could have contributed, not only the person who reported an injury. A testing program violates the rule only if it is used to penalize someone for reporting.

## Temps: who tests?

When you use a staffing agency, the agency is the employer of record and typically runs pre-assignment and post-incident testing under its own policy. Agree with your partner on:

- Which assignments require pre-assignment testing and which panel is used
- How reasonable-suspicion and post-incident tests are triggered on your site
- How results are communicated and kept confidential
- What happens when a worker is removed from an assignment

*This article is general information, not legal advice. Drug testing law has many details, especially for medical cardholders and federally regulated roles. Consult employment counsel before adopting or changing a policy.*

## The bottom line

Legal marijuana did not take away an Arizona employer's right to a drug-free workplace. What protects you is a written policy that follows the state statute, uniform application, careful handling of medical cardholders and clear documentation of impairment.
