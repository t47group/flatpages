If you manage an auto auction or a dealership in the Valley, you already know your labor needs rise and fall with vehicle volume. What has changed in recent years is how unpredictable that volume has become, and how the mix of vehicles is shifting. Electric vehicles, lease cycles and supply swings in the used market all feed directly into how many drivers, porters, detailers and lot attendants you need on a given day.

This piece takes a qualitative look at those forces and what they mean for staffing.

## Used-car supply moves in waves

Used vehicle supply follows new vehicle sales and leasing with a lag of several years. When new car production and leasing slowed earlier in the decade, it reduced the number of late-model vehicles returning to the market a few years later. When new sales and leasing recover, more vehicles eventually flow back through lease returns, fleet disposals and trade-ins.

For auctions, that means sale-day volume can swing noticeably from one season to the next, and sometimes from one week to the next. For dealerships, it affects how many trade-ins and auction purchases flow into reconditioning.

The staffing implication: **your baseline crew should match your steady volume, and flexible labor should cover the swings.** Carrying a full crew for peak volume year-round is expensive. Running lean and scrambling on heavy sale days hurts throughput and customer experience.

## EVs change the work, not just the count

Electric vehicles are a growing share of what moves through Phoenix lots, and they bring some practical differences.

### Charging and battery state

An EV that arrives with a low battery cannot simply be driven across the lot. Teams need procedures for checking charge, managing chargers and staging vehicles. That adds handling time and requires workers who understand the process.

### Handling and safety training

EVs are often heavier than comparable gas vehicles, accelerate differently and can be very quiet at low speed. Lot drivers and porters benefit from specific orientation on EV handling, especially in crowded staging areas.

### Condition reporting

Inspection and condition reports for EVs can include battery health information and other EV-specific items. Teams that write condition reports need to know what to capture.

### Reconditioning

Detail and recon tasks are similar for EVs, but technicians need to know which components to avoid and which manufacturer procedures apply. Mechanical recon on EVs typically involves specially trained technicians.

## Federal incentives changed in 2025

Federal tax policy is one of several factors that can affect EV demand. According to the IRS, the federal new and previously-owned clean vehicle credits are **not available for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025**. Shifts like that can influence buyer behavior, dealer inventory decisions and, eventually, what flows through auction lanes. We are not making predictions here, but it is the kind of change worth watching as you plan staffing mix.

## Heat is a constant on Phoenix lots

Whatever the vehicle mix, auction and dealership lot work in Phoenix means long hours on hot pavement for much of the year. Arizona's heat safety guidance emphasizes water, rest, shade, training and acclimatization. Build those into staffing plans, especially for new or temporary drivers who have not yet adjusted to the conditions.

## Staffing roles to plan for

Here are the roles we most often see flex with volume in the automotive space:

- **Lot drivers and porters** who move vehicles between check-in, staging, lanes and pickup
- **Check-in and condition report staff** who document vehicles on arrival
- **Detailers and recon helpers** who prep vehicles for sale
- **Lane and sale-day support** for auctions
- **Transport coordinators and yard attendants** who keep inventory organized

Each of these benefits from a reliable core plus a trained flex pool that knows your site.

## Practical steps for auction and dealership managers

1. **Track volume by day of week and by season.** Use it to set a baseline crew and identify when you need flex labor.
2. **Train for EVs now.** Add EV handling, charging procedures and safety to your standard orientation, including for temporary workers.
3. **Verify driver qualifications.** Confirm license status and driving history requirements are met consistently for every driver, temp or direct.
4. **Plan for sale-day surges.** Ask your staffing partner to keep a pool of workers already oriented to your lot so you are not training new people on your busiest day.
5. **Reassess quarterly.** Vehicle mix and volume shift faster than they used to. Check your staffing model against actual volume every quarter.

## The bottom line

Changing used-car supply and a growing share of EVs mean auction and dealership work is less predictable and more specialized than it used to be. Building a flexible, trained crew is the best way to handle both.
