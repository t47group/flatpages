Every November we get the same set of questions from operations managers: Do we have to pay time and a half on Thanksgiving? If we close for Christmas, do hourly people get paid? Does holiday pay count toward overtime? With distribution centers running hard through the holidays and dealerships pushing year-end sales, the answers matter for both budgets and morale.

Here is what federal and Arizona law actually require, and where the overtime math gets tricky.

## The short answer: holiday pay is not required

The U.S. Department of Labor is direct about it: the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) **does not require payment for time not worked, such as vacations or holidays.** Those benefits are a matter of agreement between the employer and the employee.

The FLSA also does not require a premium rate for working on a holiday. Thanksgiving is treated like any other day for federal wage purposes. If a non-exempt employee works on Thanksgiving, you owe at least their regular rate, and overtime applies only if they go over 40 hours in the workweek.

Arizona does not add a private-sector holiday pay requirement. The state's wage laws focus on minimum wage, earned paid sick time and timely payment of wages. So for most Phoenix-area employers, holiday pay is a policy choice, not a legal mandate.

The exception is federal contract work. DOL notes that contracts covered by the Service Contract Act can carry holiday fringe benefit requirements in their wage determinations. If you hold federal contracts, check yours.

## Where holiday pay meets overtime

Even though holiday pay is optional, once you offer it, federal overtime rules shape how it works.

### Paid holidays you don't work do not count as hours worked

If you give hourly employees eight hours of paid holiday on Christmas Day while the building is closed, that pay is excluded from the regular rate and those hours do not count toward the 40-hour overtime threshold. Federal regulations treat pay for holidays not worked as not compensation for working, so it also cannot be credited toward overtime you owe.

Example: an employee gets 8 hours of holiday pay and works 36 hours the rest of the week. Total paid hours are 44, but hours worked are 36, so no overtime is owed under the FLSA. (Your own policy may be more generous.)

### Holiday premium pay can be credited toward overtime

Many employers pay time and a half or double time for working a holiday. DOL's overtime guidance says extra pay for work on holidays, weekends or rest days, at a rate of **at least one and a half times** the normal rate, can be excluded from the regular rate and **credited toward overtime** owed that week.

Premiums below time and a half work differently, so if you pay something like a flat extra dollar an hour for holiday shifts, have payroll review how it is treated.

### Holiday gifts vs. bonuses

DOL allows employers to exclude genuine gifts from the regular rate. A holiday gift qualifies when it is a reward for service and is **not measured by or dependent on hours worked, production or efficiency.** A same-amount gift card for everyone at the holiday party fits. A "work every shift through December 31 and get $200" payment does not; that is an attendance bonus, which must be included in the regular rate for overtime.

## Earned paid sick time around the holidays

Arizona's earned paid sick time law still applies during the holidays. Employees can use accrued time for qualifying reasons, including their own illness or caring for a family member. Be careful with blanket rules like "no sick time the day before or after a holiday." Policies that discourage legitimate use of protected sick time can create problems. If you see attendance patterns that concern you, talk with your HR advisor about what documentation rules allow.

## Policy decisions to make now

If you have not settled your holiday policy for this season, here is what we suggest deciding and communicating before Thanksgiving week:

1. **Which days are paid holidays,** if any, and who is eligible (full-time only, after a waiting period, and so on).
2. **What rate applies to holiday shifts.** If you pay a premium, consider making it at least time and a half so it can be credited toward overtime.
3. **How holiday pay affects overtime.** Put the rule in writing: paid holiday hours not worked do not count toward 40, unless you choose to be more generous.
4. **Scheduling and volunteers.** Decide how you will staff Thanksgiving, Black Friday, Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve, and whether you will ask for volunteers first.
5. **Temp workers.** Holiday pay for agency workers follows the staffing agency's policy and your bill rate agreement. If you want temps to receive a holiday premium, agree on it with your agency in advance so it shows up correctly on both the paycheck and the invoice.

## Why it matters on the floor

Holiday policy is a retention tool as much as a payroll rule. In a tight market, the warehouse down the road offering a holiday premium can pull people off your schedule for the busiest week of the year. Clear, consistent holiday rules, announced early, help people plan and help you staff.

*This article is general information, not legal or payroll advice. Confirm your specific obligations with your HR advisor or employment counsel.*

## The bottom line

Neither federal nor Arizona law requires holiday pay or a holiday premium for private employers. But once you offer either, the FLSA's overtime rules decide how it is counted. Decide your policy, put it in writing and make sure your staffing partner bills and pays it the same way.
