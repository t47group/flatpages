January has a way of arriving all at once. Peak wraps up, returns pile in, a new minimum wage takes effect, budgets reset and the first planning meetings for summer start before anyone has caught their breath. A little preparation in November and December makes the first quarter much smoother.

Here is the checklist we suggest Phoenix-area operations, HR and site leaders work through before January 1, 2027. Print it, share it with your team and check items off as you go.

## 1. Pay and budget

- **Apply the 2027 minimum wage.** Arizona's minimum wage is set to rise to **$15.65 per hour on January 1, 2027**, based on the CPI formula in A.R.S. 23-363. Confirm the official notice from the Industrial Commission of Arizona and update payroll systems before the first pay period.
- **Address wage compression.** Decide whether pay bands above the floor will move, and communicate changes before they take effect.
- **Update loaded labor costs.** Include payroll taxes and workers' comp in your cost per hour.
- **Confirm 2027 bill rates.** If you use a staffing partner, get updated rates in writing for any roles affected by the new minimum.
- **Check local rules.** If you operate in Flagstaff or Tucson, confirm their separate local minimums.

## 2. Posters, documents and policies

- **Replace required workplace posters** with current versions, including the Arizona minimum wage and earned paid sick time notice.
- **Review offer letters and job postings** for outdated pay rates.
- **Review your earned paid sick time policy.** Confirm accrual, caps, carryover and your benefit year align with A.R.S. 23-372 and that pay stubs show the required information.
- **Refresh your handbook.** Make sure anti-harassment, safety and reporting procedures are current and apply to everyone on site, including temps.

## 3. Hiring and verification

- **Confirm your Form I-9 edition.** Use the current version from USCIS and watch posted expiration dates.
- **Audit your E-Verify process.** Arizona requires E-Verify for all new hires. Confirm cases are created consistently and on time.
- **Plan for temp-to-hire conversions.** If you plan to convert seasonal temps in January, remember you will need to complete your own I-9 and E-Verify case for each conversion.
- **Ask your staffing partner for proof of E-Verify enrollment** if you have not done so recently.

## 4. Safety and heat planning

- **Review your 2026 incident data.** Look for patterns in injuries and near misses during peak.
- **Write or update your Heat Illness Prevention Plan.** Arizona's Workplace Heat Safety Task Force recommendations were approved by the Industrial Commission in April 2026, and ADOSH continues its Heat Stress State Emphasis Program. Use the cooler months to update your plan, using the ADOSH template if helpful.
- **Schedule heat training before May.** Include supervisors, new hires and temporary workers.
- **Plan acclimatization.** Map out how new and returning workers will be phased into hot conditions.
- **Clarify temp safety responsibilities.** Confirm in writing who provides site-specific training, PPE and injury reporting with your staffing partner.

## 5. Workforce planning

- **Run a peak post-mortem.** What went well, where were you short, which shifts had the most no-shows and how long did it take to fill gaps?
- **Forecast Q1 volume by week.** Include returns season in distribution, and auction and dealership volume patterns for automotive operations.
- **Set baseline and flex headcount.** Staff your core for steady volume and plan flexible labor for spikes.
- **Identify your keepers.** Flag top seasonal performers you want to retain, convert or bring back next peak.
- **Map automation changes.** If new equipment or systems go live in 2027, plan training and transition labor now.

## 6. Market awareness

- **Benchmark pay.** Check current BLS occupational wage data and local job postings for the roles you hire most.
- **Track the local market.** Note the Phoenix metro unemployment rate and employment trends in your industry through BLS and the Arizona Office of Economic Opportunity.
- **Watch major employers nearby.** New distribution centers, manufacturing ramp-ups and construction projects in your corridor can affect your applicant flow.

## 7. Staffing partner review

- **Review performance.** Fill rate, time to fill, attendance and early turnover for the past year.
- **Confirm insurance.** Request current certificates of insurance for workers' comp and general liability.
- **Revisit the agreement.** Check scope of work, training responsibilities, injury reporting, conversion terms and how hiring and discipline decisions are handled.
- **Share your 2027 plan.** Give your partner your volume forecast, upcoming projects and roles you expect to need, so they can build a bench ahead of time.

## 8. Legal and regulatory watch list

Keep an eye on these items in 2027:

- **Federal joint employer rule.** The Department of Labor proposed a new joint employer rule in April 2026. Watch for whether it is finalized.
- **Federal heat standard.** OSHA's proposed heat rule has not been finalized. OSHA's updated heat emphasis program remains in effect.
- **Arizona heat guidance.** ADOSH is updating its emphasis program based on the 2026 guidelines.

*This checklist is general information, not legal advice. Consult your HR advisor or employment counsel about your specific obligations.*

## The bottom line

The first quarter sets the tone for the year. Pay changes, compliance updates, heat planning and a clear workforce plan are much easier to handle in November than in mid-January.
