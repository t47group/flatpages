By the end of October, most Phoenix distribution centers have already made their big peak decisions. Headcount targets are set, shifts are drafted and the first seasonal workers are on the floor. But the next eight weeks still hold plenty of variables, and the operators who come out of December in good shape are usually the ones watching the right signals and adjusting early.

Rather than guess at a hiring number, here is how we read the current environment and what we would watch between now and New Year's.

## The Phoenix industrial market keeps filling in

Metro Phoenix has spent several years adding large amounts of warehouse and distribution space, especially in the West Valley. That space is steadily being occupied. CBRE's Q3 2026 Phoenix industrial report put vacancy at **9.7 percent**, down 150 basis points from a year earlier, with **1.59 million square feet** of positive net absorption for the quarter.

For labor planning, the takeaway is simple: more occupied buildings means more operations competing for the same pool of pickers, packers, loaders and equipment operators, often within a few miles of each other along the same freeway corridors.

## What shapes peak labor demand this year

We see a few forces worth tracking:

### Retail and e-commerce volume

Holiday order volume drives fulfillment and parcel work more than any other factor. Watch your own customers' forecasts closely and ask them how confident they are. Volume that arrives later and more compressed than planned is harder to staff than volume that arrives early.

### Large employers set the pace

When the biggest fulfillment and parcel operators in the Valley ramp up, they pull workers from everyone else. Starting pay, shift premiums and sign-on incentives at those sites shape what it takes for smaller operations to fill a shift. Keep an eye on what is being advertised along the I-10 and Loop 303 corridors.

### The local labor market

The BLS reported the Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler metro unemployment rate at **4.9 percent** (preliminary, not seasonally adjusted) for August 2026. That is a useful benchmark, but remember that warehouse labor is its own market. The real test is how many qualified people respond to your posting and actually show up for orientation.

### January wage changes

Arizona's minimum wage is set to rise to $15.65 on January 1, 2027, based on the CPI formula in state law. If any seasonal workers will carry into January, build that into your bill rates and pay plans now.

## How to use the next eight weeks

Plans made in August rarely survive November intact. Here is where we would focus:

1. **Lock in your forecast cadence.** Agree with customers and your staffing partner on a weekly volume update. Small, frequent adjustments beat big last-minute scrambles.
2. **Stage a ready bench.** Ask your agency to keep a group of screened, oriented workers available for surge days, rather than recruiting from scratch when volume spikes.
3. **Front-load training.** Get forklift evaluations, safety orientation and site walkthroughs done before the busiest weeks, when trainers and leads are stretched thin.
4. **Watch week-one attendance.** The first few days of a new hire's assignment tell you a lot. Address scheduling and transportation issues quickly, before they turn into turnover.
5. **Protect your core team.** Long overtime stretches wear down your best people. Use seasonal labor to keep core staff productive through the final push.
6. **Plan the ramp-down.** Decide in advance how and when you will release seasonal workers in January, and whether you want to keep top performers for returns season or convert them.

## Returns season is part of peak

In fulfillment, December's outbound peak is followed by January's inbound returns. Many operations under-plan for this second wave. If you staff returns processing early, you can keep strong seasonal workers engaged instead of letting them walk away in late December.

## A note on numbers

National seasonal hiring announcements get a lot of press every fall, but those figures rarely tell you much about a specific building in Tolleson or Goodyear. We would rather plan from your volume forecast, your historical attendance and what competing sites nearby are paying. Your own data beats a national headline.

## The bottom line

Phoenix's industrial market continues to absorb space, which keeps competition for warehouse labor steady through the holidays. The best peak plans are flexible: a solid forecast cadence, a ready bench, early training and a clear plan for January.
